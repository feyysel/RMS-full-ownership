## 1. The silent costs running your restaurant today

Most restaurants don't lose money in one big moment — they lose it **daily, in small leaks** that go unnoticed. Ask yourself honestly:

- **Lost and forgotten orders.** Handwritten tickets, shouted instructions, a busy Saturday rush. Tickets get lost, items get cooked wrong, remakes are free food out of your pocket.
- **Slow, bottlenecked ordering.** Every order means a waiter walking to the kitchen and back. During peak hours the pass becomes a bottleneck, tables turn slower, and customers leave hungry or annoyed.
- **Zero visibility from the owner's chair.** You can't see what's happening on the floor or in the kitchen right now. You only find out about problems when a customer complains — or a waiter quits.
- **Running on guesswork.** Which dishes actually sell? Which hours are your peak? Which waiters are your best performers? If you can't answer with numbers, you're making decisions on instinct while your competitors use data.
- **Opaque money.** Tips, discounts, voids, and cash are easy to pocket or mistrack when nothing is logged. Without records, you can't audit, and staff know it.
- **Customers who wait for everything.** Wait for a waiter, wait for the menu, wait to order, wait to pay, wait for the bill. Every wait is a reason to not come back.
- **Chaos across branches.** If you own more than one location, each branch runs on its own system — or no system — and you have no central way to compare performance.

> Every one of these is a solved problem. The question is not *if* your business is leaking — it's whether you can see it.

## 4. A day in the new flow

1. Customer sits at a table, scans the QR code.
2. They browse the full menu with photos and prices on their own phone.
3. They place the order — it appears **instantly** on the kitchen screen.
4. Kitchen cooks in priority order; status updates live (Accepted → Cooking → Ready → Served).
5. Customer sees their order progress without asking. Need the waiter? One tap on the bell button.
6. Waiter serves, tracks earnings, closes out the table — every transaction recorded.
7. The owner opens a dashboard and sees the whole day: sales, dishes, tables, staff — with one glance.
